# Candidates — Unsent: The Micro-Confession Sprint

## Method (applied to every candidate)

Each candidate is exactly two parts: a `To:` line (role / first name / relationship —
no real full names or handles) plus one message line written as it was typed, with
no explanation after it. Scored 1–5 on the judge's own criteria, in the judge's
order of importance:

- **A — Genuinely unsent:** reads as typed and never sent, not written to be sent.
- **B — Specific and honest:** concrete detail (object, place, time, habit), not general.
- **C — Felt reason it stayed in drafts:** too honest / too late / too kind / too afraid,
  felt in the words, not stated ("I never sent this because…" would fail C).
- **D — To/message interdependence:** swapping the addressee would break it.

Total /20. Gate: whole entry 1–260 chars, message line ≤ 200 chars. Pure jokes,
greetings and motivational lines are auto-rejects per the brief, however they score.

## Selection among the 20/20 scorers

Six candidates scored 20/20: #2 (piano), #23 (dented car), #27 (don't believe),
#28 (night nurse), #34 (dropped out), #40 (snow globe). Tie-breaks applied:

1. **Size (judge tie-break: smaller text wins).** #2 is the smallest of the six
   (93 bytes total vs 118–154 for the others, same counting).
2. **Confession shape.** #2 admits a wrong done to the addressee (sold her gift);
   #28 is gratitude more than confession; #27/#34 admit concealment but name the
   reason ("because…") inside the message, which costs felt-ness.
3. **Immediacy.** #2's gut punch lands in the first five words ("I sold the piano…").
4. **Ambiguity of the unsent reason (strength, not weakness).** "I still haven't
   told you" reads as too-afraid if Grandma is alive, too-late if she is gone —
   the judge can feel either; no reason is stated.

Winner: **#2**. Runner-up: **#28** (night nurse — most original addressee, kept as
the resubmit variant if the live board shows #2 underperforming). Third: **#39**
(Dad / voicemail — strongest pure-grief line, but D=4: it would fit any dead
loved one with a personalised voicemail greeting).
